Head Waters and Piper design language

Six apps share one paper, one type scale, one spacing ladder, four depths, and one way of marking what is primary, selected and focused. Each keeps its own accent, its own mark, and one shape you can spot from the top-left corner. HC VIDEO supplies the depth and the split between primary and selected. Its colours, its 14 px body text and its buttons that shrink on a phone stay with HC VIDEO.

Apply first · version 2

Copy tokens.css into an app and put data-product="silo" (or the app's own name) on <html>. Version 2 changes are listed, breaking ones first, in 04-changes-for-builders.md. The generator runs 1,099 checks, tests both ends of every gradient, and fails if any value version 1 had wrong is put back (python3 build_tokens.py --selftest).

01 Corners

You can tell the six products apart from the top-left corner alone. Every bar is the same height, in the same type, with the same radius and depth. What changes is the mark, the accent (on the primary button and the selected item only), and one shape: Stone's engraved groove under the bar, Silo's folder tab, Scythe's cut corner, Surge's airmail stripe, Piper's parchment and burgundy reed, and the four-colour garden strip on Home.

02 HC VIDEO

HC VIDEO does one thing the suite lacks. Every surface in it wears one of nine named roles from app/globals.css, and a unit test, gel-theme-guard, fails any component that paints a flat surface by hand. Its paint lives in shared classes, used in 98 of its 179 component files. Its type and spacing do not: they are Tailwind utilities written page by page. So this answers the open question of how much of its look is shared. The surfaces are shared, and the layout and type are per page.

03 Type

Seven sizes, a serif for titles and a sans for everything a person operates. The serif titles already run through Stone, Silo, Scythe and Piper, so they stay as the family's voice. Body text moves up to 16 px. The suite writes 12 px text 119 times in Silo alone, for sentences a practitioner reads between visits. The steps grow by about a quarter each, a modular scale (Tim Brown) close to Material 3's body and title sizes (type scale tokens).

  1. T1Body text is 16 px on a 24 px line, and a sentence a person reads is never smaller than 14 px.
  2. T212 px is only for uppercase eyebrows, table headers and chips, never for a sentence.
  3. T3Font sizes come from 12, 14, 16, 20, 24, 30 and 38, and one screen uses four of them at most.
  4. T4Page and section titles are in the display serif; every control, label and number is in the UI sans, including a standalone big number (--hw-type-figure, tabular).
  5. T5Numbers in a column use tabular figures and align right.
  6. T6A paragraph is at most 68 characters wide (Butterick: 45 to 90).
  7. T7Piper's dictated words are 20 px, because she reads them at arm's length while talking.

04 Spacing and grouping

Things that belong together sit closer than things that do not, and that one rule does most of the grouping. Nielsen Norman Group calls it proximity and finds it overrides colour and shape. A drawn box (common region) is the stronger tool, so keep it for when space alone cannot separate two groups of the same kind. Things that act alike look alike (similarity), which is why one filled button per view reads as the one action.

  1. S1Every margin, padding and gap is 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48 or 64.
  2. S2A label sits 8 above its field, and the field's hint sits 4 below it.
  3. S3Fields in one group sit 16 apart, and groups that hold such fields sit 32 apart at every width. 24 separates groups only when nothing inside them is more than 12 apart.
  4. S4The space between two groups is at least twice the largest space inside either of them.
  5. S5A group gets a border or a fill only when space alone cannot separate it from a neighbouring group of the same kind. A selectable option is always bounded, and grows when its name wraps, so the name and its price read as one target.
  6. S6Buttons in one row sit 8 apart, and the row sits 32 below the last field.
  7. S7Cards are padded 24 (16 on a phone), and so are page margins.
  8. S8Everything a person taps has a hit area of at least 44 by 44 px, padding included (48 dp on Android). A 36 px visible button is allowed on a pointer layout only inside that hit area. Apple asks 44 points (HIG), Material 48 dp (M3), WCAG 2.2 AA 24 px (2.5.8).

05 Depth and shape

Four depths sit above the page, each built HC VIDEO's way. The top edge is lit, the fill darkens slightly toward the bottom, and the shadow is tight close to the surface and wide and soft further away. In dark mode a shadow barely shows, so each step up gets a lighter surface as well. That is how both Apple and Material 3 show elevation in dark mode.

  1. D1There are four depths above the page: inset, card, raised and float. No component invents a fifth.
  2. D2A card never sits inside a card. What sits inside a card is an inset well or a plain list.
  3. D3Bars and docks are raised; menus, popovers and dialogs float above them; only a modal dialog puts a scrim behind itself.
  4. D4Card, raised and float surfaces all carry the 1 px lit top edge.
  5. D5In dark, each step is lighter than the one below: page #11161c, card #19202a, raised #212935, float #29323f.
  6. D6Radius is 8 on controls, 12 on cards and lists, 16 on sheets and dialogs, and full on chips and avatars. Nothing else.

06 Colour roles

Components name a role, never a hex. Sixteen shared roles and five per product carry every colour in all six apps. Contrast is a computed fact: the generator checks every ink step on every surface, every accent against its text, and every outline and focus ring against what it sits on. It fails the build if any one is short (WCAG 1.4.3, 1.4.11).

  1. C1Components write roles such as --hw-surface or bg-surface, and never a hex.
  2. C2Ink on any surface reads at 4.5:1 or more, and so does a label at the light end of its button's gradient; control outlines and the focus ring read at 3:1 on every surface, wells included.
  3. C3The accent marks four things only: the one primary action in a view, the selected item, focus, and live state. Headings, body text and icons are ink.
  4. C4Danger, warning, success, neutral and done mean status and nothing else; no product accent sits within 40° of hue of one, and any two accents stay 5.0 apart for colour-blind readers (OKLab, deutan and protan simulation).
  5. C5Text on an accent fill uses --hw-on-accent, never a hard-coded white.

07 Primary, selected, focus

Primary, selected and focused are three different states, and each has its own mark. HC VIDEO separates the first two, and Piper's restyle already keeps purple for what is active. The family rule merges both: a filled accent means "do this", a tinted accent with an edge means "this one is chosen", and a solid ring means "the keyboard is here". Colour alone never carries a state (WCAG 1.4.1).

  1. St1A view has at most one filled button, and it is the primary action.
  2. St2Selected always adds one mark that is not colour: a check, or a change of shape such as the 3 px spine.
  3. St3Focus is a 2 px solid ring in the accent's ink, 2 px outside the control, shown for keyboard focus only (2.4.13).
  4. St4A disabled primary keeps its place, and a line under it says what is missing.
  5. St5Every status chip carries an icon and a word.
  6. St6A pressed button sinks with --hw-press, as HC VIDEO's does, and keeps it under reduced motion.

08 Motion

Motion is there to show where something went: a folded step, an opened row, a dialog arriving. Three durations cover it, with one curve. The values are Material 3's standard curve and the short end of its durations (M3 tokens). Reduced motion is honoured with a media query (MDN).

  1. M1Hover and press take 120 ms, a fold or expand 200 ms, a sheet or dialog 300 ms, and leaving takes two thirds of arriving.
  2. M2Everything eases on cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1) and exits on (0.3, 0, 1, 1). Nothing bounces.
  3. M3Nothing slides farther than 16 px, and nothing loops except a live indicator such as Piper listening.
  4. M4Under reduced motion, durations are 0 and only a 100 ms fade remains.

09 Patterns every app repeats

Seven patterns cover almost every screen in the six apps. Each is drawn below in a different product, so the family shows across them.

  1. P1The mark and product name sit top-left and take the person home.
  2. P2The bar shows five destinations at most; the rest go under More.
  3. P3Theme, account and sign out live in one floating menu at the top right, not as three controls on the bar. The assistant is the same round button, bottom right, in every app.
  4. P4The current destination carries aria-current="page" and the product's signature mark: an underline, or Silo's tab.
  5. P5From 1024 px wide, a list (300 to 360 px) and its detail sit side by side; below that, the detail replaces the list and offers Back.
  6. P6A list row is 56 tall: one line of title, one of meta, and the row's next action at its right end.
  7. P7Labels sit above their fields and are names, not questions.
  8. P8The primary button ends a form. On a laptop it is right-aligned with the secondary to its left; on a phone it is full width with the secondary under it.
  9. P9An error appears at its field, says what to do, and on submit the first one takes focus (NN/g).
  10. P10A table has eyebrow headers, 48 px rows, right-aligned numbers, no vertical rules, and status as a chip.
  11. P11An empty region says what will appear there and offers the one action that fills it, inside a dashed outline. It never explains how the system works (NN/g).
  12. P12Only an action that cannot be undone asks first. The dialog's title names the thing and what happens; its buttons are verbs, never OK and Cancel; and the destructive one is red and comes last (NN/g).
  13. P13A page error says what happened in the person's words and what they can do, and offers a retry. It never shows an environment variable, a host name or a stack trace.

10 Each product's own

Each product owns one accent, one mark and one shape, each taken from the name Parker gave it. The shape is the part that lets a person tell one product from another from a corner. That matters because a practitioner will have several of these open in tabs, and the tab favicon and the top-left corner are what she glances at.

11 Applying it

Each app changes one file first, its globals.css, because the old colour names can point at the new roles; the mapping is in the header of tokens.css. What version 2 renamed, added or changed, breaking changes first, is in 04-changes-for-builders.md. Piper's native theme reads the native group of tokens.json (plain numbers, elevation layers, faces). Each repo keeps a guard test like HC VIDEO's gel-theme-guard that fails on a hex colour in a component.

The builders' questions and their answers: TOKEN-QUESTIONS.md · the review and what came of each item: 03-review-astra.md · the close calls behind this page: notes-close-calls.md · build_tokens.py · specimens.css

12 Sources

Only sources that changed a number or a rule above are listed.

  • Gestalt grouping: proximity, common region, similarity (Nielsen Norman Group).
  • WCAG 2.2: 1.4.3 contrast, 1.4.11 non-text contrast, 1.4.1 use of colour, 2.5.8 target size, 2.4.13 focus appearance.
  • Apple Human Interface Guidelines: layout, accessibility (44 pt targets), typography, dark mode, sidebars.
  • Material 3: elevation, colour roles, type scale, corner radius, easing and duration, 48 dp targets.
  • Type: modular scales (A List Apart), line length (Practical Typography).
  • Patterns: confirmation dialogs, error messages, empty states (NN/g); prefers-reduced-motion (MDN); design tokens format (W3C community group).
  • Our own, measured in private repositories: HC VIDEO's tailwind.config.ts, app/globals.css and its theme guard test; the suite's own globals.css files and layouts; the dictation app's screen files; the screenshots in the audit.
